# Strip Biocrash's HUD down to the wrist

Biocrash's HUD should shrink from six or more always-on panels to a near-empty screen. That screen holds the hotbar, the crosshair prompt and captions, plus warnings that appear only when something changes. Exact vitals, the objective, lamp charge and the full inventory move into a **wrist-link view the player raises while the world keeps running**. This is the pattern behind the best-regarded horror interfaces. Dead Space put health, inventory and locator on the suit to remove the "wall of safety" between player and game ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-designing-i-dead-space-i-s-immersive-user-interface)). Alien: Isolation made its tracker cost vision and interpretation so players felt "backed into a corner" ([AvP Galaxy](https://www.avpgalaxy.net/games/alien-isolation/develop-audience-alien-isolation-report/)). The highest-value changes are cheap. First, apply contextual show/fade rules and remove the persistent bottom-right inventory list. Second, swap `system-ui` for a deliberate two-font system. Third, replace any `backdrop-filter` over the canvas with near-opaque panels; one measured case showed CSS blur costing more GPU time than the 3D scene itself ([Oxide rack-explorer PR #68](https://github.com/oxidecomputer/rack-explorer/pull/68)). Fourth, add glyphs and have/need numbers to the crafting costs so colour no longer carries meaning on its own. Biocrash should keep its Tab recipe list, Aseprite icons and orange/teal palette, and its hidden unease level, which is the right engine for making the HUD itself untrustworthy. The bigger structural moves come later: a capped slot inventory with base storage, recipe unlocks driven by known materials, and short real-time crafts. Accessibility fallbacks are not optional extras. Biocrash needs a full-HUD toggle, pause-while-inventory-open, a UI-scale property, reduced flicker and integer-snapped icons, because going fully diegetic carries known accessibility costs.

## The priority list: twelve changes ranked by impact per hour

The table orders the work so each step stands on its own and can be polished before the next one starts. Effort assumes a solo developer working in vanilla TS/DOM over three.js r187.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contextual visibility rules for vitals, buffs, goal line and hotbar; hide-all-HUD key | Change | Low (1–2 days) | Very high: horror tone, less clutter |
| 2 | Remove always-on inventory list; fold it into the Tab screen and wrist view | Change | Low | High |

| 5 | DOM write discipline: write on change, `transform`/`opacity` bars, `contain` | Change | Low | Medium–high: frame stability |
| 6 | Wrist-link view (hold to peek, tap to pin), world keeps running, slight scene blur | Add | Medium | Very high: core identity |
| 7 | Unease-driven HUD corruption (compass drift, false toasts, flicker), with honesty rules | Add | Low–medium | High: signature horror |
| 8 | Hotbar polish: selection frame, item-name flash, stack count, durability bar, integer icon scaling | Change | Low | Medium |
| 9 | Accessibility settings: `--ui-scale`, text size, caption size and box, effects toggles, pause-inventory option, full-HUD mode | Add | Medium | High: reach and fairness |
| 10 | Real-time inventory/crafting with blur and dim; Esc stays a true pause | Change | Low | High: tension |
| 11 | Recipe unlocks by known materials + "New recipe" toast; station chips; "???" hints | Add | Medium | Medium–high: discovery |

Items 1–5 change no game systems and can ship in a week. Items 6–10 define the game's interface identity. Items 11–12 belong with the storage and slots update already planned.

## Make the screen nearly empty and the wrist link essential

The current layout breaks the main heuristic in the horror-HUD literature. One analysis of diegetic design recommends keeping simultaneous elements low and points to Dead Space's three or four ([Indieklem](https://indieklem.substack.com/p/19-the-diegetic-dilemma-benefits)). Biocrash shows vitals, an inventory list, the hotbar, a compass, an objective panel and prompts all at once. Survival-crafting games get away with more because they have more stats to track. Even so, they cluster their HUDs or reveal them on demand. Sons of the Forest packs health, stamina and a minimap into one bottom-right cluster and shows cold and low health through screen effects and short messages rather than extra bars ([Corrosion Hour](https://www.corrosionhour.com/sons-of-the-forest-ui-hud-overview-guide/)). Minecraft shows its armor and oxygen bars only when they matter and hides everything with F1 ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Heads-up_display)).

**Contextual visibility should be the first job.** Valheim players built several mods to get it, including iHUD, ImmersiveHud and Ulfs Dynamic HUD. That level of modding is strong evidence of demand in this genre. iHUD's rules make a good starting template. The health bar hides when full. Food shows only when critical. The hotbar appears on swap. Status effects show for 5 s when gained and come back with under a minute left. Everything appears while the inventory is open, hiding is done by fading alpha, and one hotkey toggles the whole HUD ([Nexus Mods: iHUD](https://www.nexusmods.com/valheim/mods/3867)). Applied to Biocrash, the health bar fades after about 4 s at full and returns on any change. Body heat stays hidden in the comfortable band and appears with a severity icon as the player trends toward cold. The buff line shows on gain and again near expiry. The goal line collapses after each update. These timings are a modder's defaults, not studio-validated numbers, so tune them by playtest. Low vitals should feed the vignette, desaturation and edge-frost effects Biocrash already has, not brighter bars. Atmosphere and information then become the same signal.

**The bottom-right inventory list should go.** Minecraft, Subnautica and Valheim all leave the inventory off the HUD. Subnautica 2's split is the model to copy. The field HUD shows oxygen, health, hunger and thirst as icons and only raises temperature warnings in extreme areas. Opening the PDA switches the vitals to exact numbers, such as how long until the player needs to eat or drink ([subnautica2.guide](https://subnautica2.guide/wikis/hud)). The top-right objective panel should collapse in the same way, into a single pulsing wrist-link icon that stays until the player checks it. Subnautica 2 does this with its AI companion's notification, and pinned recipes give players control over what stays visible ([subnautica2.guide](https://subnautica2.guide/wikis/hud)). The compass stays, but sparse: cardinal letters, a few ticks and at most one marker. A brief directional "wrist ping" can stand in for permanent waypoints, much as Dead Space's palm-projected locator line replaced a map ([Medium](https://medium.com/inbeta/dead-space-ui-design-lessons-for-vr-39aa9e976ca8)).

**The wrist link should be raised with a key, and the world keeps running.** Green Hell's smartwatch opens only while F is held and pages between nutrients, compass and time ([PwrDown](https://www.pwrdown.com/gaming/green-hell-how-to-use-the-smart-watch/)). Biocrash can use hold-to-peek and tap-to-pin. The view shows exact vitals, lamp charge as pips, the objective with recent station-core messages, and compass detail. The key choice is pausing. Fallout 3's Pip-Boy pauses the game and Fallout 76's does not ([Indieklem](https://indieklem.substack.com/p/19-the-diegetic-dilemma-benefits)). For horror the answer is not to pause. A slight blur of the surrounding scene, borrowed from Alien: Isolation's tracker, turns each check into a risk the player weighs ([AvP Galaxy](https://www.avpgalaxy.net/games/alien-isolation/develop-audience-alien-isolation-report/)). There are three pitfalls. Information needed every second must never sit only on the wrist, so critical warnings stay on screen. Text on a 3D or pixel wrist model becomes unreadable. A fully modelled device adds production cost. For a solo web game the cheap and correct first version is **a flat 2D DOM panel styled as the wrist screen**, which slides up from the bottom-left as the arm viewmodel lifts.

A later, optional upgrade puts the wrist screen in-world. The standard three.js method paints the UI into an offscreen 2D canvas and uploads it as a `CanvasTexture` used as the screen mesh's emissive map. Scanlines are added in a shader chunk so scene lighting still layers over the screen ([threejs-crt-shader](https://github.com/unframework/threejs-crt-shader)). The texture should update only on change, throttled to roughly 10–15 Hz. Pixel icons need `NearestFilter`. HTML-in-canvas would let live HTML draw straight onto a 3D surface. It is still behind a Chromium flag with announced breaking changes, so it is not something to ship on ([WICG html-in-canvas](https://github.com/WICG/html-in-canvas)). Building the wrist panel in HTML now keeps that migration path open.

## Turn the unease level against the interface itself

Biocrash's hidden unease level already drives vignette, grain and static. It should also corrupt the HUD. The precedents show unreliable interfaces work as a horror tool. Alien: Isolation's motion tracker is deliberately imperfect and "a matter of interpretation" ([AvP Galaxy](https://www.avpgalaxy.net/games/alien-isolation/develop-audience-alien-isolation-report/)). Signalis's developer said "if you're comfortable in a survival horror game, something isn't quite right" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/signalis-game-rose-engine-interview/)). Eternal Darkness is the canonical example of fourth-wall sanity effects ([DreadPixels](https://dreadpixels.com/eternal-darkness-sanitys-requiem-deployed-fourth-wall-tricks-before-anyone-could-stream-them/)); its specific effect list could not be verified for this report.

Each effect is cheap in DOM/CSS. The compass strip drifts or jitters, or briefly shows a phantom marker. The wrist-link objective text corrupts for a beat or shows a wrong line in the station core's terminal font. A false toast reports a pickup the player didn't make, or "signal detected" with no source. A health bar flickers to a false low for a frame. Hotbar icons swap for an instant. Static bands cross the HUD layer. Frequency should scale with unease. Two design rules, inferred rather than sourced, keep this from backfiring. First, **real danger readouts never lie for longer than a moment**, and any false information should be recognisable on reflection. If players stop trusting the HUD, they stop reading it. Second, the meta layer (fake system messages) stays rare, because Eternal Darkness is remembered for rare, shocking moments. Every corruption effect must sit behind the reduced-flicker setting and respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by default.

Per-frame static and compass jitter are cheaper as a `transform` on a pre-rendered strip, or as a pass in the existing post chain, than as DOM text churn. A visor-style pass (edge barrel distortion, slight chromatic aberration, faint scanlines) should apply to the 3D scene only. DOM text stays crisp on top, because distorting text harms readability.

## Inventory and crafting: keep the list, add scarcity and friction

The Tab crafting panel already follows the dominant modern pattern, a filtered recipe list like Minecraft's recipe book and Valheim's station lists, so it needs refinement rather than a rewrite. Four additions do most of the work. First, a **"craftable only" toggle**. Minecraft offers one, keeps uncraftable recipes viewable with a red button, and uses Shift to craft the maximum ([Minecraft Wiki: Recipe book](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Recipe_book)). Second, **category tabs hidden while empty** (Tools, Gear, Food, Building). Third, search once the list passes about 30 recipes. Fourth, **unlocks by known materials**. Valheim shows a recipe only once every ingredient has been picked up at least once and gates it behind a nearby station of the right level, shown as a star chip ([GameSaveLab](https://gamesavelab.com/valheim/guides/recipes-not-unlocking/)). Picking up chitin for the first time should reveal every recipe it completes, with a "New recipe" toast as in Minecraft. That keeps the early list short and makes exploring the unknown planet pay off. Biocrash's "needs lit fire" tag is already a station requirement and should become an icon chip. Locked recipes can appear as "???" silhouettes with one known ingredient, which hints without spoiling. A Forest-style crafting mat supports experimentation, but it is slower and costly to build ([The Forest Wiki](https://theforest.fandom.com/wiki/Crafting)). It belongs in a later "experiment" mode, not as the main interface.

**Inventory and crafting should run in real time, and Esc should remain a true pause.** Dead Space allowed pausing but left "inventory management... to the throes of real-time" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2008-03-12-whew-dead-space-will-let-you-pause-after-all.html)). Diegetic, non-pausing UI is credited with sustaining its atmosphere ([iABDI](https://www.iabdi.com/designblog/2022/3/18/h04cs7ub04vkmyfcs3t2krmfey5mc3)). The Tab screen should dim and blur the scene without hiding it, keep audio live, and sit on one side so creatures stay partly visible. A rummaging loop and a small bag animation signal vulnerability. Field crafts become short real-time actions of about 1–3 s with a progress ring that movement cancels, which makes crafting a risk rather than a free menu action. Queued crafting belongs at base workbenches. The Game Accessibility Guidelines warn against making precise timing essential and suggest allowing actions while paused ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). A **"Pause while inventory open" option, off by default,** meets that guideline without removing tension for everyone else.

**Unlimited stacks undercut horror and should become a capped carry inventory once storage exists.** Survival horror tension centres on "the moment you open the inventory and realize you cannot carry everything." On the other side, "Meaningful scarcity creates choices. Tedious scarcity creates errands" ([GameSpace](https://gamespace.com/all-articles/news/bullets-backpacks-and-breaking-points-how-scarcity-defines-survival-horror/)). The working models are small and legible. Resident Evil Requiem's Grace starts with eight slots that Hip Pouches expand. SIGNALIS limited the player to six stacks and later moved the flashlight out of normal slots ([GameSpace](https://gamespace.com/all-articles/news/bullets-backpacks-and-breaking-points-how-scarcity-defines-survival-horror/)). When RE Zero removed the shared Item Box, its inventory "unravelled completely" ([ihobo](https://blog.ihobo.com/2016/09/game-inventories-4.html)). That is the case for pairing a field cap with base storage. The recommendation is **about 8–16 carry slots with per-item stack caps**, expandable through crafted pouches (the hide wrap and bone needle are natural candidates), and roomier storage at base. The always-carried lamp and knife sit outside normal slots. The slot range is an inference from Requiem and SIGNALIS, not a tested number. Two models should be avoided. A Tetris grid is fiddly to build in HTML. A precise numeric weight cap pushes players toward what Thomas Grip calls a mindset of "pure numbers" ([GameSpace](https://gamespace.com/all-articles/news/bullets-backpacks-and-breaking-points-how-scarcity-defines-survival-horror/)). If weight arrives, it should be a soft state (Light / Burdened / Overloaded) affecting stamina or noise.

The interaction model should put **click-to-select plus a context action bar** (Use, Equip, Drop, Split, Store) first and drag-and-drop second. Click paths are easier to make keyboard- and controller-friendly. Familiar bindings should carry over: Shift+click to quick-transfer or craft ×5 (Biocrash already does the latter, as Valheim does, per the [Valheim Wiki](https://valheim.weirdgloop.org/w/Workbench)), number keys over a hovered item to assign a hotbar slot, and R for a stable Sort that never buries items. Green Hell's auto-sort can hide small items behind large ones ([Green Hell Wiki](https://greenhell.fandom.com/wiki/Inventory)), and Minecraft is criticised for having no auto-sort at all ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/toughest-inventory-systems-in-video-games/)). Tooltips should appear on hover and on keyboard focus with name, one-line description, stats, condition and "Used in: N recipes". Food gets a Don't Starve-style three-stage freshness bar. Don't Starve's Fresh / Stale / Spoiled stages each carry stat penalties, and its storage containers change the spoilage rate ([Don't Starve Wiki](https://dontstarve.fandom.com/wiki/Food)), which gives a later cold cache a clear purpose. Durability shows as a thin bar only below 100%. Grim survival suits condition tiers (Crude / Sturdy / Fine) better than rainbow rarity colours, though that is a judgment call rather than a sourced finding.

## Style, performance and pixels: opaque panels, two fonts, integer icons

**Typography gives the biggest visual improvement for the least work.** `system-ui` differs per OS and reads as a web page. The two-font system should be:

- a condensed tech display face for short labels and numbers (Rajdhani, Oxanium or Teko, all free Google Fonts suited to HUDs), and
- a highly legible sans such as Inter for body text and captions ([fontadvice.com](https://fontadvice.com/font-collections/video-game-ui-fonts/)).

Share Tech Mono or VT323, used only for the station core's voice, makes that character instantly recognisable. Fonts should be self-hosted as woff2 through Vite and preloaded so they don't swap in mid-game. Xbox guidance asks for a plain sans-serif option whenever a stylised font is used, and for sentence case except on one- or two-word labels ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)).

**Panels should be near-opaque, not frosted.** Oxide measured CSS `backdrop-filter` over a WebGL canvas forcing the browser to "re-composite and re-blur everything behind each panel on every frame". It cost 3.1 ms of compositor GPU time per frame, against 0.7 ms once the blur moved into WebGL ([rack-explorer PR #68](https://github.com/oxidecomputer/rack-explorer/pull/68)). Another project over a 3D scene gained 106% frame rate by dropping glass blur and decorative blur glows and moved to 85–95% opaque panels "to stay readable" ([PORTFOLIO PR #3](https://github.com/nutankumarkm/PORTFOLIO/pull/3)). Biocrash's panels should be about 85–92% opaque and dark, with a 1px rim and a static soft shadow. If frosted glass is ever wanted, it should be done as Oxide did it: one blur pass in the post chain, masked to panel rectangles that are measured on resize rather than every frame.

The DOM overlay remains the right technology for the flat HUD because of text quality, layout and accessibility, but only with **write discipline**. Never read layout in the game loop. Cache element references. Write only when the displayed (quantised) value changes. Animate bars with `transform: scaleX()` and `opacity` rather than `width`. Prefer `textContent`. Give each widget a fixed size and `contain: layout paint style` so its changes stay local ([MDN contain](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/contain)). three-perf-hud and DevTools paint flashing can confirm the HUD isn't repainting every frame ([three-perf-hud](https://github.com/iniGlowee/three-perf-hud)).

**Colour should keep orange and teal, add a danger colour, and never let colour carry meaning alone.** The warm/cool pair survives red-green colour blindness better than red/green. Xbox guidance requires pairing colour with shape, icon or text and says unavailable states need more than grey, such as a lock or a "Not available" label ([XAG 103](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)). Dead Space's light-blue holograms are the cautionary example, "a nightmare for visual accessibility" ([iABDI](https://www.iabdi.com/designblog/2022/3/18/h04cs7ub04vkmyfcs3t2krmfey5mc3)). A desaturated red or magenta can be reserved for danger, and the palette should be checked in Color Oracle.

**Pixel icons stay, but only at integer scales.** Saint11's "sacred rule" is never to mix pixel resolutions or smooth-scale pixel art. He also argues pixel UI can sit beside a realistic world when the UI is treated as its own consistent "world" ([Saint11](https://saint11.art/blog/consistency/)). Each icon should sit in the same dark slot with an orange rim so the set reads as the suit's display system. CSS `zoom` and fractional `devicePixelRatio` (Windows laptops at 125% and 150%) produce uneven pixels, which MDN calls hard to solve ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Games/Techniques/Crisp_pixel_art_look)). The fix is to compute icon size in JS: `64 * max(1, round(uiScale * dpr)) / dpr` CSS px with `image-rendering: pixelated`, at whole-pixel positions. Hotbar slots of about 72–80 px hold the 64 px icon at 1×. On switch, a bright selection frame and slight scale-up, plus the item name fading in above the bar for about 1.5 s, follow Minecraft's convention ([Minecraft Wiki](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Heads-up_display)). The stack count goes bottom-right in an outlined pixel font, and the durability bar appears only once the tool is damaged. The hotbar can dim when idle but should not hide completely, so weapon readiness stays readable.

**Accessibility baselines are concrete.** The PC hard floor is 18 px at 1080p, text must scale to 200% without breaking, and icons must scale with text ([XAG 101](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101)). IGDA recommends 32 px default text and 46 px for captions, mostly with TV distances in mind ([IGDA GA-SIG](https://igda-gasig.org/how/platform-level-accessibility-recommendations/text-size/)). Captions need a semi-opaque box plus an outline, at most 40 characters per line, two lines, mixed case, and speaker names ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/if-any-subtitles-captions-are-used-present-them-in-a-clear-easy-to-read-way/)). For Biocrash at 1920×1200 that means:

- body HUD text at 20–24 px,
- key numbers at 24–32 px,
- captions at 46 px with a 75–200% slider, and
- the station core's name tag shown in teal.

A single `--ui-scale` custom property should replace `zoom` where possible, alongside a separate text-size option. Menus need a visible `:focus-visible` ring, a focus trap in open panels, Esc to close and return focus, and remappable keys. Toggles should cover HUD glitch, chromatic aberration, scanlines, shake and bob. A **"full HUD" mode** gives a conventional fallback for players who find the minimal, diegetic layout hard to use. The Indieklem analysis recommends this kind of fallback, citing Risk of Rain 2's inaccessible on-avatar item display ([Indieklem](https://indieklem.substack.com/p/19-the-diegetic-dilemma-benefits)). The notes contain no sourced photosensitivity flash-rate limit or contrast ratio. Check the corruption effects against published flash guidance before shipping.

## Conclusion

The research points to one design idea: in Biocrash, information should cost something. A permanent bar is free to read and therefore never frightening. A wrist check that blurs the world, a craft that a creature can interrupt, a bag that cannot hold everything and a compass that sometimes lies all charge the player attention, time or certainty. That is what the best horror interfaces do. Every cost needs an accessibility escape hatch (full HUD, pause-in-inventory, reduced flicker), so fear stays a design choice and never becomes a barrier.

The order of the work also matters. The first week's changes (contextual fades, removing the inventory list, fonts, opaque panels, cost glyphs) involve no system rewrites and will make Biocrash feel deliberate rather than like a web page with a canvas behind it. The wrist link and unease corruption then give the game an interface identity of its own. The capped inventory should wait for the storage update, because scarcity without a safe place to put things turns into the "errands" that players resent.
