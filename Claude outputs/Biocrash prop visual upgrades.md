# Ground, light and layer Biocrash's props

Biocrash's props will gain the most from fixing how they sit in the scene, not from remodelling them. Three changes do most of the work, and each is a day or two for a solo dev: screen-space AO (N8AO), switching the deprecated `PCFSoftShadowMap` to the now-soft `PCFShadowMap` with tighter shadow settings, and grounding props in the shader (sink and tilt, base darkening, terrain-material blend at the base, decal splats). Once props look like they belong in the world, the next jump is a shared "material patch" layer in `onBeforeCompile`. It would add a world-space detail normal, macro tint and roughness variation, per-instance wear, wetness and moss, and fake subsurface translucency for the fungi and bulbs, all on the existing instanced pools. Emissive glow needs an HDR pipeline (a half-float composer, emissive above 1.0, mipmap bloom with a threshold near 1, then AgX or Neutral tonemapping plus a LUT) so the saturated alien hues don't clip. A dim environment map is also needed, or the hull metal renders black. The Blender pipeline changes go last because they take longest: high-to-low baking of normal, AO and curvature, trim sheets for the hull debris, CC0 scan sources, and KTX2 compression in place of base64-wrapped PNGs. Comparable games (Scorn, Subnautica, Returnal, Alien: Isolation) agree on one art-direction point: weirdness needs function, glow must mean something, and human tech should feel heavy and mundane next to the alien.

## Priority map: grounding and AO first, pipeline overhaul last

The table orders the recommendations by impact per hour for a solo dev. The effort figures are estimates, not measurements: none of the research found millisecond costs on an RTX 5070 at 1920x1200, so every performance choice below should be checked with a GPU timer.

| # | Change | Effort | Impact | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Swap `PCFSoftShadowMap` to `PCFShadowMap`, tighten frustum, use `normalBias` | Hours | High | Deprecated API; small-prop shadows detach with too much bias |
| 2 | N8AO post pass, tuned for prop-scale contact | ~1 day | Very high | The main fix for "pasted-on" props |
| 3 | Shader grounding: sink/tilt, base darkening, terrain blend, splat decals, debris skirts | 2–4 days | Very high | Removes the hard contact line |
| 4 | HDR bloom + tonemapper + LUT + dim env map + fog/hemisphere floor | 1–2 days | High | Makes glow read without clipping; metal stops going black |
| 5 | Shared material-patch utility: detail normal, macro variation, per-instance wear/wet/moss | 3–5 days | High | Kills instance sameness and the "single noise scale" tell |
| 6 | Fake SSS + rim + pulse on fungi and bulbs; light pool + ground light cards | 2–3 days | High (signature look) | Makes the alien flora feel alive |
| 7 | Wind sway, alpha-to-coverage on fibers, dithered LOD fades | 2–3 days | Medium | Brings small props to life, removes popping |
| 8 | Blender bake pipeline (normal/AO/curvature), trim sheets, CC0 scan sources | 1–3 weeks | High, but slow | Raises the asset ceiling; bigger payoff after 1–7 are in |
| 9 | KTX2 + meshopt; stop base64-wrapping binaries | 1–2 days | Medium (load/VRAM) | Matters for players on weaker GPUs |
| 10 | Godrays, LightProbeGrid bounce | Optional | Medium | "High" tier polish |

## Grounding and contact shadows fix the "pasted-on" look

**`PCFSoftShadowMap` is deprecated and must go.** The three.js migration guide states that from r181→r182 "PCFSoftShadowMap with WebGLRenderer is now deprecated. Use PCFShadowMap which is now soft as well" ([three.js Migration Guide](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Migration-Guide)). r186 then removed it. The migration guide records the removal for WebGPURenderer, and a changelog summary says renderers now warn and fall back to `PCFShadowMap` ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)). Downstream projects already treat it as gone, shipping "PCFShadowMap, not r186's removed PCFSoftShadowMap" ([skyline PR #114](https://github.com/a2f0/skyline/pull/114)). On r187 Biocrash is therefore already getting a fallback plus a console warning. Setting `renderer.shadowMap.type = THREE.PCFShadowMap` makes the code honest. Look at the result after the change: one user reported pixelated shadows after only bumping to r182 ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/shadow-pixelation-issue-in-three-js-0-182-0/88746)). The research did not confirm how `shadow.radius` and `blurSamples` behave under the new soft PCF, so tune by eye.

The bigger shadow win is configuration. A large plain `shadow.bias` lifts a small prop's shadow off its base, which reads as floating. The usual practice is a tiny bias (around −0.0001 to −0.0005) plus a `normalBias` of about 0.02–0.05 m. These are general shadow-mapping values, not from a three.js source. At night the wrist-lamp spotlight shadow matters most, because the player looks straight down it. Give it a tight cone, `near` around 0.1 and `far` around 30, and a 1024–2048 map. Point-light shadows are the trap: "each point light will render the full scene additional six times," and a per-frame dynamic one "can push FPS down to 10" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/talk-to-me-about-point-lights-and-performance/48258)). Allow the campfire at most one, at 512–1024 and updated every other frame. Sprouts, tufts and small fungi should not cast shadows; AO and decals cover them. For the open terrain sun or moon, `three-csm` (practical split mode, per-cascade map size, `shadowNormalBias`, `fade`) gives crisp near-player shadows without one huge map. It needs `csm.setupMaterial()` on every material ([three-csm](https://github.com/StrandedKitty/three-csm)). r186 also added a `SunLight` addon with cascaded shadows for both renderers ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)), which is worth testing before adding a dependency.

**N8AO is the best screen-space AO option for this stack.** It drops into pmndrs postprocessing as `N8AOPostPass` after the `RenderPass`. It has quality presets from Performance (8 samples) to Ultra (64 samples, 16 denoise), and its half-res mode is "usually 2x–4x faster" ([N8AO README](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao)). By contrast, three's SSAO, SAO and GTAO passes re-render the scene with an override material; one measured GTAOPass setup cost about double the triangles plus four full-screen passes ([Paprika3D](https://paprika3d.com/docs/guides/threejs-ssao-artifacts)). N8AO has three settings that matter here. `screenSpaceRadius: true` with 16–64 px suits a first-person camera that sits near small props. `intensity` works as a power (about 2 is subtle, about 5 strong). The `color` option can tint the AO, and a very dark teal or violet fits the alien biomes. Hardware MSAA doesn't work with it, so the README recommends an `SMAAEffect` pass afterwards ([N8AO README](https://github.com/N8python/n8ao)). That choice affects item 7: alpha-to-coverage needs MSAA. Use Medium or High at full res on the 5070, half-res Performance on weaker GPUs, and N8AO's debug mode `lastTime` to measure. Tune AO under the wrist lamp, not in daylight.

**Shader grounding removes the seam that AO alone can't.** Props read as pasted on when they have a hard contact line, no base darkening, an orientation that ignores the slope, and no transitional material. Placement-side fixes are well established: align prototypes to the slope with random rotation and scale ([Szwajka](https://medium.com/@kacper.szwajka842/gpu-run-time-procedural-placement-on-terrain-cc874e39bbfb)). For Biocrash, blend orientation between world-up and the terrain normal per prop type (tall stalks stay mostly upright, debris plates lie fully flat). Sink each prop by the largest height difference across 3–4 base samples so no edge floats.

The best idea is a three.js version of Unreal's runtime-virtual-texture mesh blending, where meshes sample the terrain's colour and normal near their base ([Polycount guide](https://polycount.com/discussion/216486/ue4-terrain-mesh-normal-blending-with-virtual-textures-guide)). Biocrash's terrain is already triplanar PBR from world position, so prop shaders can run the same sampling. Fade it in with `smoothstep(blendHeight, 0, worldY − groundY)`, with `groundY` stored as a per-instance attribute and the edge broken up by noise. The prop's base then wears the terrain's own material. Round it out with three cheap layers:

- a height-above-ground darkening gradient, which is the key fallback when SSAO is off;
- instanced soft-dark splat quads under props, with a coloured additive version under glowing ones;
- debris skirts: 3–12 pebbles, spores or small fungi biased toward the downhill side, so large props look as if they've been there a long time.

The depth-fade "soft particle" trick, which costs one depth lookup per pixel ([Joost's Dev Blog](http://joostdevblog.blogspot.com/2020/12/softening-polygon-intersections-in.html)), suits fiber tufts and alpha props. The height-based blend is better for opaque props.

## A shared material patch turns instanced pools into individuals

The two most common "procedural" tells are uniform roughness and a single noise scale. "If roughness is uniformly low, surfaces will appear mirror-like"; the fix is "a secondary high-frequency roughness map or procedural noise" ([Artivoxa](https://www.artivoxa.com/why-your-cgi-renders-look-fake-and-how-to-fix-each-problem/)). Biocrash props are mostly vertex-coloured, so they have meso-scale form but little micro or macro detail. Instead of a unique texture per prop, build one `onBeforeCompile` patch utility that injects a shared header (uniforms, varyings, noise and triplanar functions). Set `customProgramCacheKey` so each variant compiles once, and use `#define` tier flags (DETAIL_NORMAL, SSS, WIND, WET) so low tiers compile lighter shaders. It should add three layers.

**Micro layer: world-space detail normal.** Ben Golus recommends the Whiteout blend for triplanar normals: "little reason to not use the Whiteout method over the UDN method". It costs three samples. Naive mesh-tangent triplanar is always wrong ([Ben Golus](https://bgolus.medium.com/normal-mapping-for-a-triplanar-shader-10bf39dca05a)). After `#include <normal_fragment_maps>`, convert the normal to world space, Whiteout-blend a 512² tiling detail normal (about 0.5–1 m repeat on stones and logs), convert back, and fade it out from about 8–25 m to hide shimmer. Offset each projection plane's UVs so corners don't mirror visibly. A neutral-grey-centred detail albedo and a roughness-grain channel ride along in the same fetch.

**Macro layer: world-space variation.** One fetch of a 256² tileable noise at roughly 1/20 m scale shifts albedo value and hue by ±10–15% toward a dirty or pale tint, and shifts roughness by ±0.1. Neighbouring stones in the same pool stop matching.

**Per-instance layer: wear, wetness, moss, seed.** Per-instance tint is free with stock materials: `setColorAt` feeds `instanceColor`, which the shared `color_vertex` chunk multiplies into `vColor` ([three.js source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/tree/dev/src)). Pack wear, wetness, moss and seed into one `vec4` `InstancedBufferAttribute` and pass it through as a varying. If hand-writing attributes gets tedious, Troika's `InstancedUniformsMesh` turns any uniform into a per-instance value automatically ([Troika](https://protectwise.github.io/troika/three-instanced-uniforms-mesh/)). The masks then become one-liners:

- **Wetness:** darken albedo to about 55% and pull roughness toward 0.08. This reads as wet without forcing MeshPhysicalMaterial.
- **Moss, dirt or spores:** `smoothstep` of world-up dot normal plus noise. Reuse the terrain's height-blend so props and ground share one overgrowth look.
- **Edge wear:** threshold a baked curvature channel. On hull plates this reveals bright bare metal (metalness 1, roughness 0.3) on convex edges and puts rust and grime in cavities.

If the raw string patches become fragile, `three-custom-shader-material/vanilla` exposes clean outputs (`csm_DiffuseColor`, `csm_Roughness`, `csm_FragNormal`, `csm_Emissive`, and so on) ([THREE-CustomShaderMaterial](https://github.com/FarazzShaikh/THREE-CustomShaderMaterial)). That fragility is a real risk: r182 made Standard/Physical "more physically accurate," r186 reworked energy conservation, and r187 added the EON diffuse BRDF to Physical ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)). Those are exactly the internals that string patches target. Older tutorials that match `geometry.normal`-style structs will silently fail to replace on r18x.

**Fiber tufts and sprouts need their own treatment.** `alphaToCoverage` smooths alpha-tested edges but "can only be used with MSAA-enabled contexts" ([three.js Material.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/materials/Material.js)). Shadows can't use it ([issue #30462](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30462)). With N8AO recommending SMAA instead of MSAA, choose deliberately: either keep `antialias: true` for alpha-to-coverage on tufts and accept the AO interaction, or rely on alphaTest plus SMAA. Use `forceSinglePass` so double-sided vegetation isn't drawn twice. Put wind sway in the vertex shader after `#include <begin_vertex>`, weighted by height squared and phased per instance by seed or position. Apply the same patch to `customDepthMaterial`, or the shadows won't sway. A Bayer-dither discard driven by a per-instance `fade` attribute gives LOD crossfades that work with InstancedMesh.

## Glow must be HDR, meaningful and paired with real light

The bioluminescence is Biocrash's signature, and three.js punishes it in three ways when it's set up naively.

**First, emissive light doesn't light anything.** Don McCurdy puts it plainly: emission brightens only the emitting surface, so add point lights at emissive objects or bake the glow onto nearby surfaces ([Don McCurdy](https://www.donmccurdy.com/2024/04/27/emission-and-bloom/)).

**Second, bloom isolation depends on luminance.** Emissive needs "higher total luminance than the surrounding scene," and if bloom won't isolate, "the surfaces are probably not bright enough." Luminance weights green heavily (0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B), so magenta or blue glows bloom far less than cyan or green at the same intensity ([Don McCurdy](https://www.donmccurdy.com/2024/04/27/emission-and-bloom/)).

**Third, without proper tonemapping, bright values clip to the "Notorious Six" pure hues** ([Don McCurdy](https://www.donmccurdy.com/2024/04/27/emission-and-bloom/)).

The pipeline that follows from this has four parts.

1. **Composer:** `EffectComposer` with `frameBufferType: HalfFloatType`, so values above 1.0 survive to the bloom pass.
2. **Bloom:** pmndrs `BloomEffect` with `mipmapBlur` (the default; `kernelSize` and `resolutionScale` are deprecated) and `luminanceThreshold` around 1.0 ([pmndrs BloomEffect](https://pmndrs.github.io/postprocessing/public/docs/class/src/effects/BloomEffect.js~BloomEffect.html)). pmndrs notes that a threshold of 1 "ensures that ootb nothing will glow, only the materials you pick" ([react-postprocessing docs](https://react-postprocessing.docs.pmnd.rs/effects/bloom)). This gives selective bloom without `SelectiveBloomEffect`, which users report renders differently on Chrome and Firefox ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/pmndrs-post-processing-selective-bloom-renders-differently-on-chrome-vs-firefox/59433)).
3. **Emissive strength:** author fungi veins and bulbs at about 2–6 so they bloom and campfire embers at 8–20, and keep lit terrain below 1.0. The Blender exporter writes `KHR_materials_emissive_strength` whenever emissive exceeds 1.0 ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html)), and GLTFLoader reads it ([GLTFLoader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html)).
4. **Tonemapping last:** in the final pass, after bloom. Note that r18x WebGLRenderer also has a built-in `setEffects()` path that applies tone mapping itself and warns if an OutputPass is present ([three.js WebGLRenderer.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js)), so don't tonemap twice.

**Choose AgX or Neutral, not ACES, for saturated alien hues.** On the three.js forum, ACES "changes your colors, not just the intensities" and bands bright values toward six clamped hues. AgX "very intentionally aims at a low-contrast default look," and Neutral "will faithfully reproduce colors" ([three.js forum: Tone Mapping Overview](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/tone-mapping-overview/75204)). AgX plus a contrast-restoring LUT (`LUT3DEffect`) is the principled setup. Use slightly desaturated emissive hues (teal-cyan rather than #00FFFF) and let cores burn toward white while the falloff keeps the colour. Keep exposure near 1 and create darkness with low light intensities; low exposure flattens the emissive hierarchy along with everything else. Subtle grain (around 0.03–0.06 opacity) hides banding in dark gradients. That figure is an art-direction default, not a sourced value.

**Material tricks for fungi and bulbs.** Use the Barré-Brisebois/Frostbite local-thickness translucency term from GDC 2011 ([slides](https://colinbarrebrisebois.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/gdc2011-approximatingtranslucency-1.pdf)). three.js already ships it as `SubsurfaceScatteringShader`, which string-replaces `RE_Direct(...)` inside `lights_fragment_begin` to add a per-light back-scatter term ([three.js source](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/jsm/shaders/SubsurfaceScatteringShader.js)). The same patch works on MeshStandardMaterial. Feed it a baked thickness map, or for procedural fungi a fresnel-plus-height term, and tint it with the glow hue so caps light up when the wrist lamp is behind them. Do **not** use `MeshPhysicalMaterial.transmission` on instanced fungi. Any visible transmissive object makes the renderer re-render all opaque objects into a multisampled half-float target every frame ([three.js WebGLRenderer.js](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js)). Add a fresnel rim and a time-and-seed pulse to `totalEmissiveRadiance`, with a heartbeat-shaped curve for horror. Gate both by the existing Voronoi vein mask and drive everything from one shared `uTime`.

**Real light, cheaply.** Shadowless point lights are affordable only in modest numbers: "a dozen or so" went unnoticed for one developer, while another rule of thumb is "4–8 lights max" ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/talk-to-me-about-point-lights-and-performance/48258)). Allocate a fixed pool of about 6 once. Assign them each frame to the nearest glowing props and fade intensity rather than adding or removing lights, which also avoids shader recompiles. Distant glowers get an additive radial "light card" on the ground, which doubles as grounding.

**Readable darkness.** Without an environment map, "metal has nothing to mirror and draws black" ([Paprika3D](https://paprika3d.com/docs/guides/make-my-game-look-better)). A very dim, tinted PMREM environment keeps hull debris and wet caps readable; r187 rebuilt PMREM for higher-quality IBL ([utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-2026-what-changed)). Add a low hemisphere floor (dark teal sky, dark violet or brown ground) and fog matched to it, so silhouettes fade into air rather than black. For a later pass, `LightProbeGridWebGL` (r184+) bakes local irradiance on the GPU, with ranged bakes spread over frames. Baked around the camp, it would give props warm campfire bounce ([three.js LightProbeGrid docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LightProbeGrid.html)). Materials rendered before the first bake miss its light ([issue #35202](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/35202)). `three-good-godrays` adds raymarched light shafts as a pmndrs pass, but it was tested only on three 0.125–0.167 ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/three-good-godrays)). Treat it as an optional High-tier experiment.

## Comparable games say: give weirdness a function and glow a meaning

The reference games agree more on rules than on looks.

**Scorn: make the weird work.** Director Ljubomir Peklar describes a world that "deals with human anatomy in a utilitarian way," with "very few straight lines." He warns that "most people think it just needs to be weird and that's it": the living gun "still works on the same principle as a regular gun," and every part belongs to "a greater system" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/behind-the-beautifully-grotesque-art-of-scorn/)). For Biocrash, that means no straight lines or perfect circles on organic props. Stalks taper and kink, bell rims are uneven, and glowing veins should follow a flow direction from base to tip rather than random Voronoi noise.

**Subnautica: anchor alien flora to real analogues.** Its biomes keep "visual analogies to coral reefs, kelp groves" while growing stranger with depth. In its deep, dark areas "every light source is a potential trap," and the player's base "stays clean, bright and operational" as a contrast ([Game Studies: Evans](https://gamestudies.org/2404/articles/evans)). Biocrash should anchor each alien prop to a real analogue (bell fungus to bioluminescent fungi or jellyfish bells, stalkwood to horsetail or bamboo) and push only one or two traits. The campfire, backpack and hull scrap should be the warm "safe" value island against cool, untrustworthy alien glow. Its developers also tuned bioluminescence as a level parameter that shifts with depth and night ([Game Studies](https://gamestudies.org/2404/articles/evans)). Biocrash's hidden unease level could drive glow intensity and flicker the same way.

**Returnal: glow carries meaning.** It drew on deep-sea creatures that use light "to lure prey," and its glow is deliberately strong to telegraph attacks ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/04/14/creating-returnals-otherworldly-enemies-vfx-driven-tentacle-tech-and-deep-sea-inspirations/)). Emissive should mark harvestable, dangerous or luring parts, never decoration. Use one energy hue per alien family, reused in related FX and UI.

**Alien: Isolation: human tech is heavy and mundane.** It limited references to objects that existed in 1979, kit-bashed props from real hardware, and aimed for "worn, everyday, and mundane" ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/baked-in-the-past-the-lo-fi-sci-fi-of-alien-isolation/1100-6418409/)). The backpack and hull plates should come from real references (aircraft panels, rivets, stencils, cable bundles, MOLLE webbing) and tell a layered story: scorch gradient from the impact direction, torn lips, exposed ribs and wiring, half-burned stencils, then alien growth taking them back.

**No Man's Sky: constrained variation, not randomness.** It calls its process "a set of rules and math that makes the art work," with artist-authored bases and bounded changes, so results avoid becoming "a muddled mess" ([Critical Hit](https://www.criticalhit.net/gaming/what-procedural-art-means-in-no-mans-sky/)). Author three to five base meshes per prop type and vary them only within tight bounds. Give each biome a locked palette of 3–5 colours plus one emissive hue, and never randomise hue freely.

**Scatter.** Apply the same discipline the Horizon Zero Dawn-style placers use: footprint spacing via blue noise, density masks, Voronoi clumping only where it helps ("clumping of a specific prototype is not always desirable"), and layered ecotypes ([Szwajka](https://medium.com/@kacper.szwajka842/gpu-run-time-procedural-placement-on-terrain-cc874e39bbfb); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024700/GPU-Based-Run-Time-Procedural)). In practice: hero anchors first, then odd-numbered clusters around them, then contact filler. Use skewed scale (many small, few large) and leave clearings as tension spaces. Follow ecological logic: fungi in damp concavities, rocks on slopes, debris collecting downhill. The giant fungi also need human-scale detail cues, such as vein thickness or pore size, or they read as small mushrooms seen up close.

## The Blender pipeline raises the ceiling once the scene is fixed

**Bake high-to-low instead of relying on vertex colour alone.** Blender 5.3's Cycles bake API still has selected-to-active, cage, extrusion, max ray distance and a 16 px default margin ([Blender API: BakeSettings](https://docs.blender.org/api/current/bpy.types.BakeSettings.html)). The practical recipe:

- triangulate the low-poly before baking;
- set data maps to Non-Color;
- use the smallest extrusion that clears artefacts, with max ray distance about 1.5–2× extrusion ([graphics-programming.org](https://graphics-programming.org/blog/blender-baking));
- remember that an sRGB normal image is "the single most common cause" of black normal bakes ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fix-blender-normal-map-baking-errors)).

For masks with no native bake type (curvature, thickness, packed channels), route the value into Emission and bake that. The same trick avoids metal on the high-poly, which "will completely break everything" ([graphics-programming.org](https://graphics-programming.org/blog/blender-baking)). Blender bakes OpenGL (+Y) normals, which matches three.js.

Headless scripts on 5.x need to handle several API breaks: `material.use_nodes` is now a no-op, `media_type` must be set before `file_format` when saving images, and dict-style Cycles access is gone ([Blender 5.0 API notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/python_api/)). For tiny instanced props, baking AO or cavity into vertex colours keeps the current texture-free pipeline and feeds the shader's wear masks. The exact 5.x enum names (for example `VERTEX_COLORS` as a target) were not verified.

**Shape language beats noise.** Rocks look blobby when they're made from smooth noise alone. Planar boolean fractures, chipped bevels, stretched stratification noise and separate high-frequency pitting read as rock, and Blender Studio's own rock generator starts from boolean intersections ([Blender Studio](https://studio.blender.org/training/geometry-nodes-from-scratch/example-rock-generator/)). Restraint matters too: dense, even procedural detail "is more eye catchy" and exposes its artificial nature ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/234616/what-texel-pixel-density-people-use-for-aaa-first-person-games-nowadays)). Hull plates should skip noise displacement in favour of solidified, bent, bevelled panels with boolean tears and a trim sheet.

**Use trim sheets and shared materials, at modest texel density.** A Polycount practitioner suggests about 250–285 px/m is enough for first-person props seen at about 1 m, or 1024 px/m with tiling detail layers ([Polycount](https://polycount.com/discussion/234616/what-texel-pixel-density-people-use-for-aaa-first-person-games-nowadays)). That is one opinion, not a standard. Combined with the world-space detail layer, about 4–6 material sets would cover everything: a hull trim sheet, tiling rock, tiling stalkwood bark, one alien-organics atlas with emissive masks, and the hero wreck. `KHR_texture_transform` lets atlas regions share one material ([GLTFLoader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html)).

**Free scan sources.** Poly Haven and ambientCG are true CC0: any use, no attribution, and raw files may ship in a game ([Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license); [ambientCG](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/)). As of 12–13 November 2026 the full Quixel Megascans library is free again on Fab with glTF/FBX downloads, but under the Fab Standard License, which says "standalone redistribution of the assets is prohibited" ([Digital Production](https://digitalproduction.com/2026/11/13/quixel-megascans-free-again/)). A web game ships its assets to every browser, and the source doesn't say whether that counts as redistribution. Read the license before using Megascans, and prefer Poly Haven and ambientCG scans as high-poly bake sources, recoloured toward the alien palette.

**Delivery.** Export GLB, then use the free `gltf-transform` CLI to `dedup`, `prune` and `resize`, compress normals with UASTC and other maps with ETC1S, and add meshopt ([glTF Transform](https://gltf-transform.dev/)). Register `KTX2Loader` and `MeshoptDecoder`, which GLTFLoader supports ([GLTFLoader docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/GLTFLoader.html)). KTX2 stays GPU-compressed in VRAM, while WebP decodes to full RGBA. Base64-wrapping binaries inflates them by about a third, so fetch .glb and .ktx2 files directly, or at least brotli the JSON. Export tangents so baked normals match Blender's tangent space. Set vertex-colour export to Active so the existing colour data survives alongside the new textures ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html)). `KHR_materials_sheen` did not appear in GLTFLoader's documented extension list, so set sheen in code if it's used. Aseprite has no place in realistic PBR authoring, but it does suit low-resolution emissive vein masks, glyph decals and flicker sprite sheets. That is a judgement call; no sources were found on it.

## Conclusion

Biocrash's props mostly look wrong because of what surrounds them, not their meshes. Contact, light response and variation across instances fail before polycount or texture resolution does. The order that follows from this runs against intuition: start with the post stack and the shaders, and leave the Blender work for last, because a perfectly baked rock still looks pasted on without AO, a tight shadow and a terrain-blended base. A wreck with 4K bakes still reads as black holes without an environment map. Once items 1–6 are in, every asset made afterwards gets those benefits for free, and the Blender work goes into shape language and trim sheets rather than fighting the renderer.

Two strategic risks need watching. First, the `onBeforeCompile` approach sits on three.js internals that changed in r182, r186 and r187, so centralise the patches and test that chunk names still match after each upgrade. TSL node materials are the long-term way out ([TSL wiki](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/wiki/Three.js-Shading-Language)). Second, no source gave real costs on this hardware, so instrument N8AO, bloom, the shadow passes and the point-light pool from day one, and build the quality tiers around measured numbers rather than assumptions.
